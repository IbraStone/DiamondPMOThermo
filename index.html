The application

PMI approves your application before you can book the exam. Prepare to prove everything you write, because applications can be audited.

Education and experience

All experience must fall within the last 10 years, in a professional setting, and be non-overlapping.

EducationExperience leading projects
Secondary school or equivalent (EQF 4)60 months (5 years)
Associate degree or advanced technical program (EQF 5)48 months (4 years)
Bachelor’s degree or higher, or a formally mapped EQF 6 qualification36 months (3 years)
Bachelor’s or postgraduate degree from a PMI GAC-accredited program24 months (2 years)

How months are counted

PMI counts calendar months, not projects. If Project I runs January to March and Project II runs February to May, you get five months total (January to May), not seven. Record each project separately, and leave out routine, operational, or administrative tasks.

What qualifies

Leading and directing a temporary initiative in a professional setting. It can be unpaid.

What does not

School projects, academic research, personal events, and home improvement.

The 35 training hours

You need at least 35 hours of project management training that matches the exam content outline. Providers can include PMI Authorized Training Partners, employer programs, universities, and self-paced courses with an end-of-course assessment. One classroom hour counts as one training hour. Books and practice exams alone do not count.

An active CAPM waives the 35 hours. This workshop is delivered by Diamond PMO, a PMI Authorized Training Partner.

If you are audited

You will need to document your education and verify your experience and training. Keep your degree records, project dates, supervisor contacts, and the course certificate from this workshop.

Tuesday and Wednesday: Ibrahim reviews each PMP application one on one. Bring your draft experience entries on Tuesday morning.

Choosing an approach

About 40% of exam questions are predictive and about 60% are agile or hybrid, so you need to recognize which approach a scenario is using.

PredictiveAdaptive (agile)Hybrid
Fits whenScope can be settled early; work is large, regulated, or costly to redoRequirements and the solution are uncertain and likely to changeSome parts are uncertain and others are stable, or deliverables can be split across teams
PlanningMost planning is done up frontA clear vision up front; requirements are refined as work proceedsPredictive planning for stable parts, adaptive for uncertain parts
ChangeHandled through formal change control and reviews between phasesExpected; the backlog is reprioritized using stakeholder feedbackFormal for baselined parts, flexible for backlog parts
DeliveryPhases, sometimes delivered in incrementsShort iterations, often 1 to 4 weeks, each ending with a demonstrationDepends on the part of the work
ExampleA medical device with regulatory phase gatesSoftware with changing customer needsA custom home: uncertain design, predictable build

  • Risk

  • Initiating
  • Planning
  • Executing
  • Monitoring and controlling

Remember that the exam follows the three outline domains (People, Process, Business Environment), not these seven. The two structures overlap, which is why the study map links each outline task to the right PMBOK sections.

Agile essentials

Agile favors people and collaboration over heavy process, a working product over thick documentation, working with the customer over contract negotiation, and responding to change over following a fixed plan. The common practices in the Agile Practice Guide (section 5.2, p. 64):

  • Backlog planning and refinement: keep the list of work ordered by value and clarify the next items.
  • Iteration planning: the team chooses what it can finish in the next short cycle.
  • Daily coordination: a short meeting to share progress and surface blockers.
  • Demonstrations or reviews: show finished work and gather stakeholder feedback.
  • Retrospectives: the team inspects how it works and agrees on improvements.
  • Shipping increments: release usable pieces early so value arrives sooner.

A servant leader on an agile team removes impediments and creates safety for the team to speak up (Agile Guide 4.2 and 4.3, p. 40–45).

Earned value cheat sheet

Applies mostly to predictive work. The full table is in the PMBOK Guide, Table 5-1 (p. 207). PV is planned value, EV is earned value, AC is actual cost, and BAC is budget at completion.

Terms to know

Organizational process assets (OPAs)
Your organization’s policies, templates, and lessons learned that a project can reuse.
Enterprise environmental factors
Conditions outside the team’s control that affect the project, such as regulations, culture, and market.
Baseline
The approved version of scope, schedule, or cost that you measure progress against.
Tailoring
Adapting the approach and processes to fit the project and organization.
Backlog
An ordered list of work still to do, reprioritized as you learn.
Iteration and increment
An iteration is a short work cycle; an increment is a usable piece of the product delivered.
Risk, issue, impediment
A risk may happen; an issue has happened; an impediment blocks the team’s progress.
Change control
The process for reviewing, approving, and recording changes to baselined work.
Retrospective
A team meeting to review how the work went and agree on improvements.
Governance
The structure, rules, and oversight that guide decisions and escalation on a project.

Questions and answers

Quick answers about the application, the workshop, and the exam. Open a question to read the answer.

Application

Do I have to use this workshop for my 35 hours?

No. Training can come from several providers, including PMI Authorized Training Partners, employers, universities, and self-paced courses with an end-of-course assessment. This workshop counts, but it provides about 20.25 hours, so you need about 15 more.

Can I count books or practice exams as training hours?

No. Books and practice exams alone are not accepted as commercial training.

I hold an active CAPM. Do I still need the 35 hours?

No. An active CAPM gives you a 35-hour credit, as long as you meet the education and experience requirements.

What if two of my projects overlapped?

PMI counts calendar months once. Two projects running in the same months count only for those months.

Does school or personal project experience count?

No. School projects, academic research, personal events, and home projects do not qualify. Experience must be leading and directing projects in a professional setting, within the last 10 years.

What happens if my application is audited?

You must provide documents that verify your education and experience, and your training is also subject to audit. Keep records of your degree, project dates, and course certificates.

What should I bring on Tuesday?

The exam

How long is the exam and how many questions are there?

180 questions in 240 minutes. 170 are scored and 10 are unscored pretest questions mixed in. There are two 10-minute breaks.

Can I go back to questions after a break?

No. Once you review your answers and start a break, you cannot return to the previous section.

Which question types appear only at a test center?

Matching, enhanced matching, point and click, and pull-down list. Case or scenario, graphic-based, and multiple-choice questions appear in every format.

Can I take it online?

Yes, with a proctor. It requires system checks and a longer check-in, and you may be asked to show ID again after a break.

What if I do not pass?

You can attempt it up to three times within one year. After three attempts, wait one year before reapplying.

How do I keep the certification?

Earn 60 professional development units every three years and pay the renewal fee.

The exam

The 2026 PMP exam tests how you apply project management in realistic situations, not how well you recall definitions.

180 questions

170 scored plus 10 unscored pretest questions, mixed throughout.

240 minutes

Two 10-minute breaks. The first comes after the case-study section; you cannot go back after a break.

Question types

TypeWhat to expect
Case or scenario (new)A detailed situation, sometimes with charts, followed by a series of questions
Graphic-based (new)Interpret a chart, diagram, or table, then answer
Multiple choice and multiple responseOne or several correct answers
Matching, enhanced matching, point and click, pull-downDrag, click, or select. Test center only

How to take it

Take it at a Pearson VUE test center (recommended) or online with a proctor, which needs system checks and a longer check-in.

If you do not pass

You may attempt the exam up to three times within your one-year eligibility period. After three attempts, wait one year before reapplying. If the year ends first, you reapply with no waiting period.

After you pass

Earn 60 professional development units every three years and pay the renewal fee.

Be careful with anyone who guarantees you will pass. PMI investigates misleading claims.
